My best gluten-free baking tips

Welcome to the crew. These are the tips I actually reach for on every bake — the ones that fix most gluten-free and vegan problems before they start. Start here, then dig into the deep dives.

If you take nothing else from this page, take the first three. They fix more bad bakes than anything else I know. Everything after that is the next layer — click through when you want the why.

1. Weigh your flour

Cups lie. A "cup" of gluten-free flour can swing 30 grams depending on how you scoop, and 30 grams is the difference between a moist muffin and a hockey puck. Buy a $12 scale and weigh in grams. This single habit is the biggest jump in consistency you'll ever make. More on why texture starts here: flour handling for better texture.

2. Let the batter rest

Gluten-free flours drink slowly. Mix your batter, then walk away for 20–30 minutes before you bake. The flours hydrate, the grittiness disappears, and the crumb sets up soft instead of sandy. Free step, big payoff.

3. Bind it right — flax or psyllium, not more gum

When a GF bake crumbles, the instinct is to dump in more xanthan. Don't. Ground flax and psyllium husk hold structure without the gummy, gluey texture too much gum leaves behind. Here's the full breakdown of what actually holds a bake together: binders for better gluten-free baking.

4. Know your leaveners

Baking soda and baking powder are not interchangeable, and using the wrong one (or too much) is why bakes taste soapy or collapse. Quick rule of thumb here: baking soda vs. baking powder — and the bigger picture in leaveners in gluten-free baking.

5. Add the baking powder last

Baking powder starts working the moment it hits moisture. Whisk it into your dry mix last, right before the wet goes in, so you don't spend its lift stirring. Small tweak, better rise: why I add baking powder last.

6. Don't skimp on the fat

Fat is what makes gluten-free taste rich instead of dry and cardboardy. And the fat you pick matters — a runny nut butter and a stiff one bake completely differently. Read the jar before you scoop: don't skimp on the nut butter, and the wider role of fat in gluten-free baking.

7. Grease the pan like you mean it

Nothing ruins a good bake like leaving half of it stuck in the tin. There's a right way to do it that gets a clean release every time: how to grease your pan like a pro.

8. Bake to temperature, not to time

Ovens run hot, cold, and uneven, and gluten-free bakes hide their doneness. A cheap instant-read thermometer tells the truth: most cakes and muffins are done at 205–210°F in the center. Stop guessing.
