How to Make Oat Milk at Home (Without the Slime)

Two ingredients, one minute, pennies a batch. The whole trick to oat milk that isn't slimy is three things: cold water, a short blend, and a gentle strain.

Skip to recipe ↓

Put oats in water, blend, strain. That's oat milk. A carton at the store is a few dollars and a list of ingredients you can't pronounce; this is two ingredients and about sixty cents.

But almost everyone who tries it once gets a slimy, gluey batch and gives up. That's not oat milk being difficult — it's three small mistakes, and once you know them it's foolproof.

Why Oat Milk Goes Slimy

Oats are full of starch and beta-glucan — a soluble fiber that turns gummy when it's warmed or worked too hard. That's great in a bowl of oatmeal and terrible in a glass of milk. So the entire game is keeping that starch calm:

Heat activates it. Friction from the blender warms the mix, and warm oats release starch fast. Use cold water — even throw in a couple ice cubes — so the batch stays cold from start to finish.

Over-blending activates it. The longer the blades run, the more oat breaks down and the more starch leaches out. 30 to 45 seconds on high is all you need. Longer is not better here.

Squeezing activates it. When you strain, let it drain on its own. Wring the bag hard and you force the slimy starch right through into your milk.

Cold, short, gentle. Get those and it comes out clean and pourable every time.

Homemade Oat Milk

Yield

About 4 cups

Total time

5 min

Keeps

4–5 days

Equipment

  • Blender
  • Nut milk bag, fine-mesh strainer, or a clean thin tea towel
  • A jar or bottle for storage

Ingredients

IngredientAmountWeight
Certified gluten-free rolled oats1 cup90g
Cold water (plus a couple ice cubes)4 cups960ml
Vanilla extract (optional)1 tsp—
Salt (optional)1 pinch—
Neutral oil or a few cashews (optional, for creamier)1 tsp / small handful—

Less water (3 cups) makes a creamier, more barista-style milk; more water makes it lighter. Use certified gluten-free oats if you need it to be GF — regular oats are cross-contaminated with wheat.

Instructions

  1. Blend cold and short. Add the oats and cold water (with a couple ice cubes) to the blender. Blend on high for 30 to 45 seconds — no longer. If you want it creamier, blend in the oil or cashews now.
  2. Strain once, gently. Pour through the nut milk bag or strainer and let it drain. Do not wring or squeeze hard — that pushes the starch through and makes it slimy. A little pulp left behind is fine.
  3. Flavor. Stir in the vanilla and a pinch of salt. That's it — no gums, no oils-and-emulsifiers, no "natural flavors." You don't need what the cartons add.
  4. Store and shake. Pour into a jar and refrigerate. It keeps 4 to 5 days. It will separate as it sits — that's the sign there's nothing fake holding it together. Shake it well right before every pour.

Common Failures and Fixes

It came out slimy. One of the three: the water wasn't cold, you blended too long, or you squeezed the bag. Next batch: ice-cold water, 30 seconds, and let it drain on its own.

It's too watery. Either under-blended or too much water. Blend the full 45 seconds, or drop to 3 cups of water for a richer milk.

It separates in the fridge. Not a failure — real oat milk with no emulsifier separates. Shake before pouring. If you want it to hold together longer, the cashews or teaspoon of oil help.

It's foaming or gritty in coffee. A splash of oil in the blend gives it more body and helps it behave like barista milk. Cold milk into hot coffee, and give it that shake first.

Two ingredients, one minute, and it costs a fraction of the carton. Once you've made it a couple times you'll never think about it again — it just lives in your fridge.
