## Explaining a mechanism

> Cocoa powder is mostly fat and flavor compounds packed into dry particles. Add it dry and those particles never fully open up — you get flat, one-dimensional chocolate. Hot liquid breaks them open and releases everything locked inside. That's the difference between good chocolate and great chocolate.

> A 12–18-hour fridge rest is the key. Gluten-free flour needs overnight rest. When you mix, starch granules need time to hydrate and absorb water into their matrix fully. Your dry ingredients, like binders and starches, also need to settle into a uniform suspension.

> Gluten-free crumb is dense, and heat moves through it more slowly than wheat. Your surface bakes fast while the interior stays damp and gummy. Even worse, carryover cooking is more pronounced in GF goods.

## Heading off the misread

> One thing to be clear about, because it trips people up: the fridge test is not a preview of the oven. Your bake doesn't go in the fridge. The cold isn't the point — it's just the honest way to read the jar.

> Doesn't make it taste like coffee — makes the chocolate taste more like chocolate.

## Giving an instruction

> Use an instant-read thermometer to ensure the correct internal temperature. The $15 investment is well worth the results. Pull at 210–215°F (depending on product).

> What most people miss is that the amount of liquid used to bloom MUST be removed from elsewhere in your recipe. For example, if you bloom 2 Tbsp cocoa with 1/4 cup water and your recipe calls for 1 cup water, only add in 3/4 cup. Otherwise, your batter will be too wet.

> Don't use it alone — blend 25–30% black with 70–75% Dutch-process. Gives you that deep color and Oreo-style flavor.

> Mix until ingredients are just combined, and remember that undermixing is better than overmixing.

## Saying what he personally does

> Here's the test I use: put the jar in the fridge for an hour, then pull it out and scoop.

> Natural peanut and almond butter — the kind with an oil layer on top you have to stir in — firm up when cold and give you real structure. These are the ones I build around. Sunflower seed butter behaves the same way and is my go-to when something needs to be nut-free.

> How I built my own:
> - Bread: Sorghum + rice + tapioca + xanthan (stretch, structure)
> - Muffins: Rice + tapioca + cornstarch (soft, moist, tender)
> - Cookies: Rice + potato starch, minimal binders (flaky, delicate spread)
