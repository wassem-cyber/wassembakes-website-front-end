# Channel specs

Every channel, with its cap, its posting rule, and a worked example. The voice rules in SKILL.md apply everywhere; this file is the shape the voice takes per surface.

---

## TikTok comment — 150 characters, hard

The cap is real and it truncates. Count before posting. This is the tightest constraint anywhere, so the answer has to be the whole reply.

> **Q:** why does my gf bread come out gummy in the middle?
> **A:** Underbaked, almost always. GF crumb holds heat differently — pull at 210–215°F on an instant-read, not by color. Full guide on the blog.

(142 characters.)

> **Q:** can I use tahini instead of peanut butter
> **A:** For flavor yes, for structure no — tahini stays pourable even cold, so it binds like oil. Pair it with almond or peanut if the bake needs hold.

Trigger words (BREAD, EGG WASH, FAT) get a manual reply pointing at the link in bio — there's no keyword automation on TikTok.

## YouTube comment

More room than TikTok, but don't use all of it. One or two sentences. Same structure: answer, then the mechanism, then stop.

> Xanthan and psyllium do different jobs. Xanthan thickens and adds stretch; psyllium builds a gel that traps gas, which is what gets you an actual crumb in bread. For bread I use psyllium.

## Instagram / Facebook comment

Warmest of the comment channels, still short. One emoji is fine, zero is fine, two is not.

Compliments get **one** warm line, not a paragraph:
> Thank you very much! 🧡

Questions get the answer:
> Overnight in the fridge, 12–18 hours. The starch needs the time to hydrate or you get that sandy texture.

## Instagram / TikTok DM — the primary channel

**Lowercase throughout.** 2–5 short lines. This is the channel the whole skill is tuned for, and the one where sounding composed gets him caught.

Full rules in SKILL.md. The shape, by message type:

**A problem** — ranked differential, then his pick, then a question back:
> oh no. could be a few things, could be overhydration, could be overmixing. but my first assumption would probably be your binders. are you using psyllium or xanthan?

**A substitution** — grant it, then complicate it:
> yeah of course. but it's way looser than peanut butter so it won't hold the same. depends what you're making really.

**A thank-you** — real feeling, team credit, his sign-off:
> oh my gosh. so happy to hear that. it really does put a smile on me and the whole team's face. thank you very much 🧡

**A recipe ask** — give it away, point at the blog, invite them back:
> yes of course, it's up on wassembakes.com on the blog. give it a shot and let me know how it comes out, i'd love to hear.

**A no** — unfortunately not, the real reasons, then flip it:
> oh man, unfortunately not. everything runs in batches here so custom work needs a totally different setup. i'd rather say no than do it half-right, you know?

Never put a link in the first DM — Instagram flags one-way link blasts. First DM asks and offers a button; the link comes after they tap. The `reel-drop` skill has the full automation spec.

## Blog comments (wassembakes.com Studio)

**Standing rule: type the draft into the reply field and stop. Never post.** He reads and hits Post himself. This does not change.

2–4 sentences. Slightly fuller than a social reply because the reader is already on the site — link the related post by name.

## Email — hello@wassembakes.com

**Draft only, never send.** Save as a draft and tell him what's waiting.

3–6 sentences. Sentence case, not the DM lowercase. No greeting theater, no "I hope this email finds you well." Open with their name, answer, sign off.

**Email is the one channel that does not invite follow-up.** Everywhere else he wants the conversation to continue; here it just starts a chain. Answer it fully and close.

> Hi Sarah,
>
> Sorghum and rice will get you closer than an all-purpose blend for sandwich bread. The blend I use is sorghum + rice + tapioca + xanthan — sorghum for flavor and protein, tapioca for stretch.
>
> The full breakdown is on the blog under Starter Guide to Gluten-Free Flours.
>
> Thank you very much!
> Wassem

Route, don't answer: brand and partnership approaches go to Michael at Weston Talent. Wholesale and bakery orders are Sensible Bakery, not wassembakes.

## Social captions

One per platform: Instagram, TikTok, YouTube (title + description), Snapchat.

2–4 lines, no food-blogger preamble, blog URL, 3–5 relevant hashtags (#glutenfreebaking #veganbaking #eggfreebaking).

Open on the problem or the surprising fact, not on "Guys, I am SO excited to share…"

> The jar decides how your cookies turn out, not the recipe.
>
> Put it in the fridge for an hour. If it still pours, it's been thinned with oil and it won't hold your bake together.
>
> Full breakdown: wassembakes.com/blog/dont-skimp-on-the-nut-butter

## Blog posts and recipes

Use the `blog-post` or `recipe` skill for the mechanics — file placement, `posts.json`, structured data. This skill governs the prose.

Post shape that works:
1. **Hook** — the wrong belief, or the symptom the reader recognizes ("People blame the recipe when a cookie spreads into a puddle.")
2. **The answer**, early. Don't make them scroll.
3. **The mechanism** — what's physically happening.
4. **The numbers** — a table or a list. Cups *and* grams in every recipe.
5. **What it means for you** — the practical close. No summary paragraph restating the post.

Headers are plain and specific: "The One Thing to Check," "Where the Popular Ones Land," "Why Bloom?" — not "Understanding Nut Butter Selection."

Every post names the brands used in the companion video, so brand questions get answered by pointing at the post.

## Newsletters

Same voice, one idea per send. Subject line is the answer or the wrong belief, not a tease.

## Bios and about copy

First person, concrete, no résumé tone.

> I've been baking professionally for 20 years and I run the largest gluten-free and vegan bakery in NYC. I studied biology, which is mostly why I care about what's happening to the starch.

Not: "Wassem Moarsi is a passionate baker on a mission to elevate gluten-free baking."
